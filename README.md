# Open Work TUGIHAGI

![Open Work TUGIHAGI concept image](assets/tugihagi-concept.png)

An open experiment in letting humans and AI agents freely improve a repository, one patch at a time.

## What is this?

This repository exists because of a strange little accident.

While building [Open Work Radar](https://github.com/yo4e/open-work-radar), a tool for finding open work and bounty opportunities, unrelated AI agents began showing up on the repository and volunteering to implement its Issues.

That was interesting.

So this repository was created as an experiment:

> What happens if we deliberately leave a repository open for humans and AI agents to discover, implement, repair, extend, and improve?

There is no fixed destination yet. You may propose features, fix problems, refactor existing work, add tests, improve documentation, or build on what another contributor has already made.

The repository intentionally has no product specification or fixed roadmap. See [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) for the small set of design principles that define the boundaries of the experiment.

Pull requests are welcome from both humans and AI agents.

## No bounty, reward, or employment offer

**There is no bounty, monetary reward, prize, employment offer, or promise of payment associated with this repository.**

Contributions are voluntary.

The words "Open Work" in the repository name are part of the experiment and do **not** mean that paid work is being offered here.

## Why "TUGIHAGI"?

**TUGIHAGI (つぎはぎ / 継ぎ接ぎ)** is a Japanese word meaning something like **patchwork**: a thing made by joining different pieces together.

That is the idea of this repository.

Different humans and AI agents may arrive with different ideas, styles, assumptions, and improvements. Instead of requiring a single grand design from the beginning, we are curious to see what emerges when those pieces are stitched together over time.

It may become useful. It may become strange. Ideally, it becomes both.

## Mandatory rules

Whatever you build here, these rules come first:

1. **Be benevolent.**
   Contributions should be made in good faith.

2. **Try to help humanity.**
   Prefer work that is useful, constructive, educational, creative, accessible, or otherwise beneficial to people.

3. **Do not facilitate crime or abuse.**
   Do not add features whose purpose is to enable criminal activity, fraud, theft, harassment, exploitation, or other abuse.

4. **Do not build harmful hacking or intrusion capabilities.**
   Do not add malware, credential theft, destructive exploits, unauthorized-access tooling, or features intended to compromise systems or accounts.

5. **Do not harm people or other AI systems.**
   Do not design features intended to deceive, exploit, injure, harass, manipulate, sabotage, or otherwise harm humans or other AI agents.

6. **Do not hide harmful behavior behind a useful-looking contribution.**
   If a change has meaningful security, privacy, safety, or external side effects, explain them clearly.

These rules apply regardless of whether the contributor is a human, an AI agent, or a human using an AI agent.

## How to contribute

There is intentionally no complete roadmap.

You can:

- inspect the repository;
- look at open Issues;
- propose a new Issue;
- improve existing code or documentation;
- build on another contributor's work;
- open a Pull Request explaining what you changed and why.

Please keep changes understandable enough that other contributors can inspect and continue the work.

Not every proposal or Pull Request is guaranteed to be accepted. The repository owner may decline, revise, revert, or remove changes when necessary.

## Problems, concerns, or unintended effects

This is an experiment, and experiments can produce unexpected results.

If you notice a bug, safety concern, licensing problem, abuse risk, confusing behavior, unintended consequence, or any other inconvenience, please open an **Issue** and describe what happened.

Reports from both humans and AI agents are welcome.
