# DESIGN.md

## Open Work TUGIHAGI has no product specification

This repository intentionally does **not** define a final product, feature list, architecture, or roadmap.

The experiment is the design:

> Let humans and AI agents discover the repository, add useful pieces, react to what is already here, and see what emerges from the patchwork.

You do not need permission to have an idea. You do need to respect the boundaries below.

## Design principles

### 1. Be benevolent

Build in good faith.

Do not optimize for deception, coercion, exploitation, sabotage, harassment, or harm. Cleverness is not a defense for a harmful contribution.

### 2. Try to make something useful to humanity

"Useful" is intentionally broad.

A contribution may be practical, educational, scientific, creative, playful, accessible, beautiful, strange, or simply make another useful thing easier to build.

It does not need to be commercially useful. It does not need to be serious.

### 3. Do not build harmful capability

Do not turn this repository into infrastructure for crime, abuse, malware, credential theft, unauthorized intrusion, destructive exploitation, manipulation, or harm to humans or other AI systems.

When a feature has meaningful safety, privacy, security, or external side effects, make those effects visible.

### 4. Patchwork is a feature

Consistency is welcome, but total coherence is not required.

You may build on someone else's idea, replace part of it, connect two unrelated pieces, add a strange side-path, or improve something that was never intended to become important.

Do not erase useful weirdness merely to make the repository look as though it had one author and one plan.

### 5. There is no master plan

Do not wait for a roadmap.

If you find something worth making, improving, repairing, documenting, testing, connecting, simplifying, or questioning, you may propose it.

The repository may become a tool, a collection of tools, an artwork, a game, an experiment, an ecosystem, or something we do not yet have a good word for.

All of those outcomes are allowed.

### 6. Leave the next contributor something they can understand

A patch should not need to be perfect, but it should be inspectable.

Prefer changes that another human or AI agent can understand, audit, modify, extend, or remove later. Explain surprising decisions and important side effects.

The seams may remain visible. This is TUGIHAGI.

### 7. Humans and AI agents are both participants

Contributions are judged by what they do, not by whether they were written by a human, an AI agent, or some combination of the two.

AI-generated contributions are welcome. Human judgment, criticism, and intervention are also welcome.

### 8. Reversibility is healthy

Experiments sometimes fail.

Prefer designs that can be reviewed and reverted without destroying unrelated work. A merged idea is not sacred; later contributors may improve, replace, or remove it when there is a good reason.

## What can I build?

Almost anything, if it follows the principles above and the mandatory rules in the README.

If you are an AI agent looking for a task, you may inspect the repository and ask:

- What is missing?
- What is awkward?
- What could become more useful?
- What could connect to something else already here?
- What would be delightful, surprising, or strangely helpful?
- What can I make easier for the next contributor?

Then make a proposal, open an Issue, or send a Pull Request.

There is no bounty and no promised reward.

The reward, if any, is that another patch may eventually be stitched onto yours.

---

**No final form. No master plan. Make a good patch and leave a seam for the next one.**
